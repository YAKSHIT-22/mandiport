# How to compare mandis before you sell

The mandi with the highest quoted price is often not the one that pays you most. Distance, quality, arrivals on the day and the charges deducted at the yard all change what reaches your hand. This guide sets out a method you can follow in a few minutes.

## Step 1: Start from take-home, not the headline

Work in one number: what you expect to be left with after moving the produce.

> take-home = price per quintal × quintals − transport cost

Transport cost is usually easiest to think about per kilometre per quintal, including the return trip and loading. If a tempo costs ₹6,000 for a 100 km round trip carrying 20 quintals, that is ₹3 per km per quintal. Use your own figure: rates differ by vehicle, season and region.

An example. Market A quotes ₹2,600 and is 120 km away. Market B quotes ₹2,500 and is 15 km away. At ₹3 per km per quintal and 20 quintals:

- Market A: (2,600 × 20) − (120 × 3 × 20) = ₹52,000 − ₹7,200 = **₹44,800**
- Market B: (2,500 × 20) − (15 × 3 × 20) = ₹50,000 − ₹900 = **₹49,100**

The "worse" price pays ₹4,300 more. The [best mandi tool](/tools/best-mandi) does this arithmetic for every market that reported your crop recently, and ranks them.

## Step 2: Check the date on every price

A price is a fact about a day, not a standing offer. Before you compare, look at when each market last reported. If one figure is from yesterday and the other from last week, you are not comparing like with like. Markets that report irregularly are riskier to travel to.

## Step 3: Match variety and grade

Markets report by variety and grade, and the difference between them is often larger than the difference between markets. If Market A's high price is for a variety you do not grow, it is not your price. Crop pages list the varieties reported, and market pages show what that specific yard has been trading.

## Step 4: Think about arrivals, not just price

The data does not include quantities, but you can reason about them. Heavy arrivals in a market usually soften prices that day; light arrivals can lift them. Local knowledge matters here: if three villages nearby are harvesting the same week, expect pressure. If a nearby market is closed for a holiday, expect its produce to arrive somewhere else.

## Step 5: Count the charges

Deductions vary by state and commodity, and they are not in the price data:

- market fee or cess
- commission agent's charge
- loading, unloading and weighment
- packing material, if the buyer does not supply it
- waiting time, if the auction is slow

Ask before you travel. A yard that charges one per cent less can be worth more than a ₹20 difference in the quoted rate.

## Step 6: Decide on timing separately

Once you know where, decide when. Three honest questions:

1. **Can the crop wait?** Onion and potato store for weeks in the right conditions; tomato and leafy vegetables cannot.
2. **What does storage cost?** Rent, shrinkage and quality loss are real costs, and they compound.
3. **What has happened recently?** The price history tool shows how the price has moved over 30, 90 and 365 days, and, once we hold twelve months of data for a market, which months have historically averaged highest there.

Past patterns describe the past. They are a reason to be careful, not a forecast. Mandiport never predicts prices, and you should be wary of anyone who does.

## Step 7: Sanity-check an unusual price

If one market is far above the rest, check:

- **How many markets reported that day?** One high market among three is noise; one among fifty is a signal.
- **Is it the same variety and grade?**
- **Is the maximum price close to the modal?** A large gap suggests only a small, high-quality lot fetched that rate.
- **Is the date recent?**

## A five-minute routine

1. Open the crop page and read the national median and its date.
2. Open **Prices by state** and find your state's median.
3. Open the [best mandi tool](/tools/best-mandi), enter your pincode, quantity, transport rate and how far you are willing to go.
4. Look at the top three results and check each one's price date and distance.
5. Open the market page for your first choice and see how often it reports and how prices have moved there.
6. Call the yard or your agent to confirm today's expected rate and charges before loading.

## What Mandiport will not tell you

We do not know your crop's quality, your buyer relationships, your storage capacity or your cash needs. Those often matter more than a hundred rupees of headline price. Use the figures to narrow the choice, then use your own judgement, and confirm before you transact.
